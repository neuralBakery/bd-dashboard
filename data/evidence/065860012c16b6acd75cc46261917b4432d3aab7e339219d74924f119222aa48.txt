										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<img width="640" height="336" src="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-1-1024x538.jpg" class="webfeedsFeaturedVisual wp-post-image" alt="Bendigo Cup Day Wins" style="display: block; margin-bottom: 10px; clear: both; max-width: 100%;" decoding="async" loading="lazy" srcset="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-1-1024x538.jpg 1024w, https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-1-300x158.jpg 300w, https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-1-768x403.jpg 768w, https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-1.jpg 1200w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 640px) 100vw, 640px" /><p>RACING</p>
<p>Bendigo trainers struck hard and early on local Cup day last Wednesday, landing the first two winners on the card, led by a pair of impressive youngsters.</p>
<p>Jarrod Robinson was the first to make his presence felt with highly-touted three-year-old making a stunning return to racing by winning the RSN Bendigo 945AM 3YO Maiden Plate (1000m).</p>
<p>He was quickly followed into the winner&#8217;s circle by Arthur Pace, whose three-year-old colt Hydro Rush continued his impressive rise through the grades with a second straight win in the Catanachs Jewellers 3YO Benchmark 64 Handicap (1300m).</p>
<p>A dominant all-the-way win for Running Rich was as much a relief as it was a delight for Robinson.</p>
<p>The gelding, who was ridden by local jockey Chelsea Jokic, has taken his time to break his maiden, albeit has contested some high-calibre races.</p>
<p>He finished second in his second start in the $250,000 VOBIS Gold Rush on Golden Mile day earlier this year and was third in the $125,000 VOBIS Gold Elvstroem Classic at Swan Hill at his next run, before going to Flemington.</p>
<p>Despite winning a trial at Seymour earlier in the month, Robinson conceded he was a little unsure how Running Rich would fare first-up on Cup day.</p>
<figure id="attachment_30038" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-30038" style="width: 600px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img decoding="async" class="wp-image-30038" src="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/brh_9295_94684.jpg" alt="" width="600" height="400" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-30038" class="wp-caption-text">Hydro Rush, ridden by Damian Lane, wins the three-your-old benchmark 64 handicap at Bendigo on Cup day.</figcaption></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>&#8220;It was more of a relief than anything, but it was a nice prizemoney race for the owners,&#8221; he said.</p>
<p>&#8220;The bar was set pretty high for him in his first prep, racing in some strong races.</p>
<p>&#8220;Even though it was a strong maiden (on Cup day), it&#8217;s probably the easiest race he&#8217;s run in since his first start.</p>
<p>&#8220;It was good to see him get it done, as he&#8217;s not fully wound up. He&#8217;s still got improvement to come.</p>
<p>&#8220;He&#8217;s not really a 1000m horse either, he&#8217;ll be better when he gets out over a bit more ground.</p>
<p>&#8220;But we&#8217;ve always had a good opinion of him, which is why we set him for the Gold Rush.</p>
<p>&#8220;He&#8217;s come back a bigger, stronger horse, so hopefully he&#8217;s got a few nice wins in him.&#8221;</p>
<p>Robinson was quick to praise a superb front-running ride by Jokic.</p>
<p>&#8220;Chelsea rides work for me every morning; very dedicated, a good rider and has good hands,&#8221; he said.</p>
<figure id="attachment_30038-2" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-30038-2" style="width: 600px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img decoding="async" class="wp-image-30038" src="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/866159678_1572325994_95319.jpg" alt="" width="600" height="750" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-30038-2" class="wp-caption-text">Arthur Pace. Photo: Racing Photos.</figcaption></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>&#8220;It was good to reward her with the ride and she did it well.</p>
<p>&#8220;He&#8217;ll probably go to Ballarat (next week), there&#8217;s a VOBIS race there for him, so Chelsea will stick with him.&#8221;</p>
<p>Robinson said the longer-term plan with Running Rich was far from set in stone, albeit it would be hard not to think the youngster was more than capable of adding to the young trainer&#8217;s handful of previous city winners.</p>
<p>&#8220;We sort of planned to miss the spring and come back when the better horses were on their way to the paddock and try and win a few races and target the VOBIS races,&#8221; he said.</p>
<p>&#8220;He&#8217;s got all the bonuses available to him.</p>
<p>&#8220;Was nice to get a win a s we haven&#8217;t had a lot of runners this season.&#8221;</p>
<p>Robinson and Jokic reignited their winning combination only a few days later, taking out the first race on the program at last Saturday&#8217;s Wycheproof Cup meeting with Ruthless Soul.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, Hydro Rush made it three wins from four starts with a near all-the-way win in the three-year-old benchmark 64.</p>
<p>The son of Headwater was quick to take up the lead after jumping from gate one and, despite looking vulnerable at times in the straight, hung tough for a half-length win under the guidance of gun jockey Damian Lane.</p>
<figure id="attachment_30038-3" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-30038-3" style="width: 600px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="wp-image-30038" src="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/bh5_9076_77785.jpg" alt="" width="600" height="486" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-30038-3" class="wp-caption-text">The Matthew Enright-trained The Muffin Man flashes home for third behind Itsukushima on Cup day. Photo: Brett Holburt/Racing Photos.</figcaption></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Hydro Rush, who debuted with a win on his hometrack in August, has been beaten only once his career at his second start at Sandown.</p>
<p>His win on Cup day was a huge thrill for the Pace family, including trainer Arthur and daughters Courtney and Patrice.</p>
<p>&#8220;A small field, full of tactics – it was probably a bit unusual seeing him being that handy in the run,&#8221; Courtney said.</p>
<p>&#8220;He handled it really well.</p>
<p>&#8220;I thought there might have been a little bit more speed on the outside – that&#8217;s what the maps suggested.</p>
<p>&#8220;Seeing him there, there wasn&#8217;t much option for him to do anything else.</p>
<p>&#8220;I liked the way he controlled the race and kicked on with it. It was tough.</p>
<p>&#8220;He was (up for the fight). He looks the part, but I think he&#8217;s got the ability to go on with it as well.</p>
<p>&#8220;A great ride and I&#8217;m just so happy for dad and the owners here too and my sister Patrice.</p>
<p>&#8220;For our hometown Cup day, we&#8217;re over the moon.&#8221;</p>
<figure id="attachment_30038-4" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-30038-4" style="width: 600px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="wp-image-30038" src="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/865108076_1572326094_94978.jpg" alt="" width="600" height="751" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-30038-4" class="wp-caption-text">Jarrod Robinson. Photo: Racing Photos.</figcaption></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Whatever the immediate future might hold, the stable is convinced the best is yet to come.</p>
<p>&#8220;He&#8217;s a very immature horse when you really stop and have a look at him. He&#8217;s still probably six months away,&#8221; Courtney Pace said.</p>
<p>&#8220;He&#8217;s still a colt and he was a little bit cheeky outside (last Wednesday), so there might be some improvement, but who knows.</p>
<p>&#8220;When he&#8217;s a baby like this, it&#8217;s just a step by step run. We&#8217;ll work out it when we get home and see how he pulls up.&#8221;</p>
<p>The Pace team further had the honour of flying the flag for5 the locals in the feature event – the $500,000 Group 3 Bendigo Cup (24000m) – with Raging Bull.</p>
<p>Third in last year&#8217;s Cup, the eight-year-old gelding finished in the prizemoney in seventh spot behind So You Are, beating home several well-fancied runners despite his 100-1 odds.</p>
<p>Another local to catch the eye on Cup day was the Matthew Enright-trained The Muffin Man, who finished third in the Prestige Event Hire Benchmark 70 Handicap (1600m).</p>
<p>The five-year-old gelding, ridden by Luke Currie, was last on the home turn, but charged home to finish one-and-half-lengths from the winner Itsukushima in a nice audition for a potential start in this Sunday&#8217;s $70,000 Ararat Cup (2000m).</p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<img width="640" height="336" src="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-3-1024x538.jpg" class="webfeedsFeaturedVisual wp-post-image" alt="Bendigo Cup Day" style="display: block; margin-bottom: 10px; clear: both; max-width: 100%;" decoding="async" loading="lazy" srcset="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-3-1024x538.jpg 1024w, https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-3-300x158.jpg 300w, https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-3-768x403.jpg 768w, https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/tng-04-nov-3.jpg 1200w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 640px) 100vw, 640px" /><p>Bendigo has embraced local Cup day after the Bendigo Jockey Club delivered another outstanding day of racing, fashion and entertainment.</p>
<p>The day was bolstered by a boost in crowd numbers, with 6868 racegoers attending last week&#8217;s event at the Bendigo racecourse, representing an increase of more than five per cent on last year&#8217;s attendance.</p>
<p>It reinforced the event&#8217;s position as one of regional Victoria&#8217;s premier sporting and social events.</p>
<p>BJC chief executive officer Paul Scullie said the strong attendance continued the event&#8217;s recent growth.</p>
<p>He said the club was delighted by the overwhelming support from the local community, members, corporate partners and visitors who helped create a fantastic atmosphere across the racecourse.</p>
<p>On the track, the $500,000 Group 3 Ladbrokes Bendigo Cup attracted an exceptional field of local, interstate and internationally trained stayers, with several runners chasing an opportunity to secure a position in the Group 1 Lexus Melbourne Cup, run earlier this week.</p>
<p>It was the Bjorn Baker-trained So You Are, ridden superbly by Rachel King, who claimed the prestigious Group Bendigo Cup, scoring his fourth consecutive victory and further strengthening the Bendigo Cup&#8217;s reputation as an important pathway to the Melbourne Cup.</p>
<p>An impressive victory over Ballarat Cup-bound Berkeley Square and Litzdeel earned So You Are a 1kg weight penalty and a confirmed start in the Melbourne Cup, highlighting the significance of Bendigo&#8217;s position on the Spring Racing Carnival calendar and its ability to shape the field for Australia&#8217;s most iconic race.</p>
<p>Scullie could not have been happier with how the overall day unfolded.</p>
<p>&#8220;To welcome 6868 people through the gates, representing another increase in attendance, is a fantastic result and a real reflection of the support we continue to receive from the Bendigo community,&#8221; he said.</p>
<p>&#8220;We are incredibly grateful to everyone who came along and helped make the day such a success.</p>
<figure id="attachment_30038-5" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-30038-5" style="width: 600px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="wp-image-30038" src="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/864366355_1863781471_25269.jpg" alt="" width="600" height="843" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-30038-5" class="wp-caption-text">Fashions on the Field proved as popular as ever on Bendigo Cup Day. Photo: Bendigo Jockey Club.</figcaption></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>&#8220;The quality of this year&#8217;s Bendigo Cup field was exceptional, and to have So You Are win in such impressive fashion with a potential Melbourne Cup start on the line reinforces just how important our race is within the Spring Racing Carnival.</p>
<p>&#8220;We were also thrilled with the standard of fashion across the course and, importantly, the number of people who supported our local businesses in preparing for the day.</p>
<p>&#8220;That broader economic contribution is something we are extremely proud of and will continue to build upon.&#8221;</p>
<p>Away from the track, racegoers once again embraced the occasion, with an outstanding display of fashion, particularly through the Beck Legal Fashions on the Field competition.</p>
<p>The club was especially encouraged by the number of patrons supporting Bendigo&#8217;s local fashion, beauty and retail businesses in preparation for the day, reinforcing the importance of Cup day to the broader business community.</p>
<p>Complementing the outstanding racing and fashion was a vibrant program of entertainment, headlined by internationally acclaimed and local violinist Evangeline Victoria, whose captivating performance added to the atmosphere of the day.</p>
<p>Racegoers were also treated to an exceptional range of food and hospitality offerings from local businesses, including Two Sisters, Ms Batterhams and Madame Cue.</p>
<p>Scullie said a particular highlight was the on-course cafe and food court operated by Year 9 and 10 VET hospitality students from Weeroona College, providing young people with invaluable hands-on hospitality industry experience while showcasing the club&#8217;s commitment to supporting local businesses, education and community partnerships.</p>
<p>The economic contribution of Bendigo Cup Day extends well beyond the racecourse and generates $3.2 million in local economic activity, supporting accommodation, hospitality, retail, tourism and other businesses throughout the region.</p>
<p>With attendance continuing to grow, BJC chairman Jack Lyons said the club anticipated another significant economic contribution from the 2026 event, further demonstrating the importance of Cup day to the local economy and community.</p>

<p>While the imported nine-year-old gelding is an undeniable long shot at 100-1, the thoughts and will of punters and bookmakers will not dim the excitement and anticipation for the former Singapore trainer’s premiership winner, who has been based in Bendigo for a little over 18 months following the demise of racing in that country.</p>

<p>Having brought him over from the UK the last two years, Ellison and connections elected to leave Onesmoothoperator behind permanently in Australia after last year&#8217;s Cup, handing over the training reins to Fitzsimmons.</p>

<p>“The old man (dad Martin) would have been pretty chuffed if he was still here. I like to think he will be looking down from heaven.</p>
<p>“And the staff here have done a great job of getting him to the line.</p>
<p>“It’s not easy with these older horses.</p>

<p>The last was Fordyce, trained by the late George Symons, in the year of At Talaq’s win.</p>

<p>“Hopefully he can do Bendigo proud,” he said.</p>

<p>“It hasn’t been easy – it’s been a bit of a challenge.</p>
<p>“But I’m probably happier with him in the last week than I have been in the last 12 months, so that’s a positive going into the biggest race of the year.</p>
<p>“He’s a nine-year-old, so he’s not getting any younger, but he’s still full of beans … bright and well.</p>
<p>“Hopefully we get to Tuesday and he can run the race of his life.”</p>
<p>Veteran Bendigo trainer Rod Symons, whose late father George trained Fordyce into 19th place in the 1986 Cup, couldn’t be happier to see the Bendigo-trained Melbourne Cup starter drought end.</p>
<p>“I’m not a young fellow anymore when you’re talking 40 years,” said Symons, who was the stable foreman back in 1986.</p>
<p>“My younger brother was on strapping duties.</p>
<p>“But it’s good for Bendigo to finally have another cup runner.</p>
<p>“We were hoping Josh Julius might have got there with Super Girl a few years ago, but it didn’t happen.</p>
<p>“Hopefully Onesmoothoperator can run well and do a lot better than us.”</p>
<p>Super Girl, a city winner for Julius and placed in the Listed Andrew Ramsden (2800m) at Flemington, a race in which the winner is given a ‘golden ticket’ into the Melbourne Cup, was humanely euthanised after breaking down in the 2020 Bendigo Cup, while attempting to qualify for the Melbourne Cup.</p>
<figure id="attachment_30038-8" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-30038-8" style="width: 608px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="wp-image-30038" src="https://static.timesnewsgroup.com.au/prod/uploads/sites/24/2026/11/DSC_0037_11725.jpg" alt="" width="608" height="405" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-30038-8" class="wp-caption-text">Tim Fitzsimmons and Onesmoothoperator. Photo: Kieran Iles.</figcaption></figure>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>“It was a totally different race back then (in ’86); you could get in with a locally bred horse, Symons said.</p>
<p>“Fordyce was bred out here at Lockwood. (Trainer) Geoff Murphy came up and had a look at him as a yearling and said I’ll give you $80,000. We paid $20,000 for the mare in foal.</p>
<p>“She was in foal with a new stallion called Sir Tristram.</p>
<p>“At the time we were taking punt on a cheapie, but I’m so glad we didn’t sell him.</p>
<p>“We had a lot of fun with him. We won the Creswick Stakes, which was then over 2000m, as a three-year-old, which qualified him for the Melbourne Cup run.</p>
<p>“It takes a lot more than that to get into a Melbourne Cup these days, but very happy for Tim to be there.”</p>
<p>Symons, now 64 and with a handful of horses in work, hailed Fitzsimmons as a breath of fresh air.</p>
<p>“He has done a great job and it’s great he’s based himself in Bendigo,” he said.</p>
<p>“He’s happy with the facilities here, has a great team around him and has got some nice horses.</p>
<p>“Onesmoothoperator does all of his gallops on the course proper, so he’s been given every opportunity here.</p>
<p>“We’re all hoping to see him do well on Tuesday.”</p>
